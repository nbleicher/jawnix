Metabolism 1960

The four essays

  1. Ocean City — Kikutake
  2. Space City — Kurokawa
  3. Towards Group Form — Maki & Otaka
  4. Material and Man — Kawazoe

Two thousand copies. Ninety pages. Sold at the door of the World Design Conference for ¥500. This book is the desk’s copy of that pamphlet: same doctrine, different inventory.

Plate 02

The School

学派

World Design Conference, Tokyo · 11–16 May 1960 · mapped to a ping tree

JAWNIX buys attention, takes a form or a call, and sells that lead. Ping: ZIP and vertical, no PII. Bid: 1800ms. Post: the full record to the winner’s webhook. Exclusive is one buyer. Shared is two to five. Returns are bad data, not a missed close. The 1960 school is the drawing method for that desk.

Category peers already run this machine: QuinStreet, EverQuote, Modernize, QuoteWizard, NextGen. We use their nouns. Architecture is one plate of analogy, not the product.

Metabolism is the name of the group, in which each member proposes further designs of our coming world through his concrete designs and illustrations. We regard human society as a vital process — a continuous development from atom to nebula. The reason why we use such a biological word, metabolism, is that we believe design and technology should be a denotation of human society. We are not going to accept metabolism as a natural process, but try to encourage active metabolic development of our society through our proposals. Metabolism 1960: The Proposals for New Urbanism — opening statement

Why the word

Noboru Kawazoe used shinchintaisha (新陳代謝) while talking through Kiyonori Kikutake’s Marine City: the exchange of materials and energy between an organism and the world outside it. In Japanese the word carries replacement — the old yielding to the new — without nostalgia for the old’s appearance. Kikutake looked the word up. The English was metabolism.

They had first considered calling themselves the Burnt Ash School, after the firebombed cities. They refused visual historicism. They kept a Buddhist idea of regeneration and a Japanese practice of rebuilding: the Ise Shrine, dismantled and remade on adjacent ground every twenty years. The sacred rocks remain. The timber turns. That is the doctrine. Not a pagoda motif. Not a rising-sun mark. A rate of change.

Kenzo Tange was mentor, not member. At CIAM ’59 in Otterlo he showed Kikutake’s Tower-shaped City and Sky House. At MIT he set a Boston Harbor studio that became the rehearsal for the Tokyo Bay plan: a linear city of looping highways and A-frame housing, artificial land thrown across water because the ground of the old city could not metabolize fast enough. In 1961 he put the plan on NHK. It was never built. It remains the clearest drawing of a city that refuses a finished state.

Fumihiko Maki, writing Investigations in Collective Form (1964), named three ways buildings have reason to be together: compositional form, megastructure, and group form. He coined megastructure in that text — “a large frame in which all the functions of a city or part of a city are housed” — and then warned against it. If the mega-form goes obsolete, it is a weight around the neck of urban society. Group form grows by linkage. The parts know why they cluster. JAWNIX keeps both: a desk that is a frame, a market that is a group form. We do not compose leads on a poster grid and call it a system.

Why it is timeless

Orthodox modernism used a mechanical analogy: the building as machine, finished at handover. Metabolism replaced it with an energy process found in all life — cycles of change, the renewal and destruction of tissue. Kurokawa later moved the same thought toward symbiosis: opposites held in an intermediate space, the en of the engawa, without forcing a compromise that erases either side.

The thought does not date because perishable units and permanent duties do not date. A shrine rebuilds. A capsule rusts. A lead expires. The question is always the same: what must last, what must be replaced, and whether the joint was designed so replacement can actually happen.

Why this desk

JAWNIX manufactures qualified demand and vends it as graded inventory. A lead is a short-life cell on a ping tree: ZIP and vertical go out with no PII; buyers bid in 1800ms; the winner gets the record by webhook. Exclusive is one dock. Shared is two to five. The desk — compliance, data, reputation — is the long-life core. Operators in insurance, home services, legal, education, and finance take those cells into their own frames. The metaphor is almost too exact. That is why it must be treated with the seriousness of a 1960 plate, not a mascot.

Works held as law

Nakagin Capsule Tower

Kurokawa, Ginza, 1972. Two cores, 140 units, 2.5 × 4.0 m, four high-tension bolts, 1.3 m porthole. Shafts imagined at sixty years; capsules at twenty-five to thirty-five. None were ever swapped. Demolished 2022. The idea outlived the object.

Sky House

Kikutake, Bunkyo, 1958. A square living floor on four concrete panels, kitchen and bath as movenettes. A children’s room later hung below, child-sized door between. The platform stayed. The service units moved. Seven adjustments in fifty years.

Helix City

Kurokawa, 1961. Double helix as a third spatial system — not only vertical, not only horizontal. “Just as in the chromosomes (DNA), the helix acts as a space frame for data transmission.” Compact city; forest kept. Superstructure recycles substructure.

Floating City, Kasumigaura

Kurokawa, 1961. And Kikutake’s Marine City, 1958: a city that floats free of a nation, multiplies by cell division, and sinks itself when aged. Artificial land on water. Industry on the rim. Housing as cylinders into the sea.

Yamanashi Press Center

Tange, Kofu, 1966. Sixteen cores, five metres in diameter, on a 17 × 15 m grid. Horizontal trays of press, studio, office. Cores cut at unequal heights so growth remains visible. Expanded 1974. The unfinished top is a sentence.

Osaka Expo ’70

Tange’s Festival Plaza under a space-frame roof. Kikutake’s Expo Tower: cabins on a ball-joint stem. Kurokawa’s Takara Beautillion, assembled in six days, flange ends left open. Toshiba-IHI: tetrahedra that could grow in fourteen directions. Apotheosis — and the last carnival before the oil crisis.

This book is not Archigram. Archigram was pop, mechanic, information, the comic of the gadget. Metabolism was social structure, Buddhist regeneration, and the rates at which a city may change. We do not borrow their lightning bolt. We borrow their joint.

Plate 03

Manifesto / Positioning

宣言

JAWNIX buys media, takes form fills, inbound calls, and live transfers, and vends those leads through a real-time tree. Ping without PII. Bid. Post to the winner’s CRM. Exclusive or shared. Returns for disconnect, wrong ZIP, missing consent. We will not accept a dead number as a natural historical process.

The frame

The frame is what must not turn over with the stock. Compliance. Provenance of data. The reputation that lets an operator dock a unit without inspecting the factory every morning. The methods by which a human inquiry becomes a graded good. Kurokawa gave the Nakagin shafts sixty years. We give the desk the same duty: outlast every capsule that hangs on it.

The frame is not a brand campaign. It is artificial land — Kawazoe’s concrete slab, ocean, or wall onto which units plug. Operators do not buy our personality. They buy access to a wall that holds.

The capsule

The capsule is the perishable unit of qualified demand. It is manufactured, graded, and vended. It has a social lifespan, not only a mechanical one: the window in which an inquiry is still an inquiry. After that it is tissue the organism has already shed.

A capsule is meaningless without what it holds — Kurokawa’s distinction between a coffee cup and a spacecraft. A rupture in the unit (bad origin, silent consent, a dead telephone) destroys the internal climate. The operator and the unit depend on each other for the duration of the dock. Then the unit is replaced. The operator remains.

What we are

  • A digital lead vendor. Same pipe as QuinStreet, EverQuote, Modernize, QuoteWizard, NextGen.
  • Media in. Form, call, or live transfer. Ping → bid → post to a CRM.
  • Exclusive (one buyer, higher CPL) or shared (two to five, speed-to-lead under a minute).
  • Insurance, home services, legal, education, finance. Exact. Unsentimental.

What we are not

  • A bro shop, a growth-hack stall, or a lifestyle agency.
  • A SaaS toy that “unlocks pipeline.”
  • A robotics company, a protocol, or a Tokyo souvenir.
  • A promise that inventory does not expire.

Plate 04

Rates of Change

代謝周期

Kurokawa divided Nakagin into three components because they die at different speeds: the ferroconcrete shafts, the moveable capsules, the service equipment. A brand that uses one clock for all three is not metabolist. It is merely modular.

He was careful: the capsule’s lifespan was social, not mechanical. Tenants would change. Needs would change. The shaft could remain. The same distinction governs a lead. The telephone still rings after the inquiry is dead. The record still exists after the exclusive window has closed. We do not confuse the survival of a row in a table with the survival of demand.

Ise rebuilds every twenty years on adjacent ground. The rocks stay. The timber is new. Tange and Kawazoe were invited to the 1953 rebuilding. They took from it a method, not a motif: regeneration without visual historicism. JAWNIX may not quote a shrine in a mark. It may quote the cycle.

Nakagin’s capsules were never replaced. One reason was structural: about thirty centimetres between units, brackets below, four bolts above. A single capsule could not come out without the ones over it. The joint had been drawn as replaceable and built as stacked. This book treats that failure as law. If a grade, a contract, or a routing rule cannot be swapped without dismantling the desk, it is not a capsule. It is a tray poured into the core.

Plate 05

Voice

語法

Write as Kurokawa wrote the Capsule Declaration: in articles, not slogans. Specify the unit. Name the joint. Do not cheer. Do not console. The reader is an adult buying perishable goods.

Three movements — ka, kata, katachi

Kikutake, after the physicist Mitsuo Taketani, designed in three stages. Ka: the general system. Kata: the abstract image. Katachi: the solution as built. JAWNIX speech follows the same descent. Do not begin a letter with the built unit if the system has not been stated. Do not leave a system hanging without a unit the operator can dock.

  • Ka — We buy media and vend leads through a ping tree.
  • Kata — Exclusive home/roof, ZIP 606xx, $240 CPL, 18-hour hold, posted to your webhook.
  • Katachi — NX-4401 won exclusive at 240. ACK 201 in 412ms. Consent on file. Losers never saw PII.

Rules of the mouth

  • Sentences are short enough to stamp on a capsule plate.
  • Claims carry a number, a grade, or a clock. If they cannot, they wait.
  • No exclamation. No emoji. No tilde of warmth.
  • Japanese may appear as texture and as the word 新陳代謝. It is not garnish and not a translation of “innovation.”
  • We do not say “cells” in public copy unless the plate is this book. In the market the unit is a capsule or a lead. The biology stays in the doctrine.
  • Humour, if any, is dry and structural. Never cute.

Combinations

Primary pair

Béton + Sumi. The shaft and the ink. Most pages, most objects. Washi as the paper the plate is printed on.

Joint pair

Joint on Béton or Washi, never as a flood field larger than a flange. If the page is mostly rust, the joint has swallowed the frame.

Life pair

Verdigris only with Sumi, Steel, or Washi. Never next to Joint in equal weight. One accent per plate.

Paper pair

Bone is one step lighter than Washi: the drawing laid on the catalogue. A plate is Washi or Bone, not both as equal fields. Mark boards, diagrams, and alt chapters sit on Bone; the book’s outer sheet is Washi.

Forbidden palettes: Swiss red-black-white as a system; purple-black startup night; neon cyan; cherry-blossom pink; gold-and-black “Tokyo luxury.” If a colour cannot be poured, bolted, or oxidized, it is not ours.

Plate 09

Grid & Growth

群造形

Not a Swiss poster grid. Maki’s three forms are the law: compositional form is rejected for the market; megastructure is the desk; group form is how operators and units cluster.

Compositional form — rejected

Buildings (or leads) arranged as isolated objects on a plane, related after the fact by alignment. This is how a mediocre media kit looks: cards equally spaced, a hero, a footer. JAWNIX does not compose inventory as a still life. A lead that is only “placed” has no reason to be next to another lead.

Megastructure — the desk

A large frame housing functions. Tange’s joint cores; Kurokawa’s shafts; this book’s left-hand core. The danger, which Maki named, is obsolescence of the whole. So the frame must be service, compliance, and reputation — things that can accept new trays — not a campaign slogan poured as architecture.

Group form — the market

Units that have reasons to be together and grow by linkage. Hillside Terrace over twenty-five years. A buyer in home services links to a grade and an origin, then to a second origin, then to a shared cluster when exclusive yield dries. The page grows the same way: a chapter is a cluster, not a column count.

The increment

The spatial module is the Nakagin capsule: 2.5 × 4. All hanging units — tickets, cards, data strips — start from that ratio. The core is 1.0 wide. Clear space is 2.5. A page may add units; it may not invent a new ratio because a layout “needs air.”

Artificial land is the horizontal slab: a béton band on which mockups sit, a letter’s two black cores, the cover’s joint line. Helix increment is rotational, not linear: a diagram may turn 15° to show growth, never to decorate.

Page anatomy

  • Left core: persistent contents. Permanent. Do not hide it for “immersion.”
  • Trays: chapters slot between unseen cores, like Yamanashi floors.
  • Flange: every section ends able to accept another section.
  • Group: applications cluster; they are not a twelve-column gallery.

If you find yourself counting twelve columns, you have left the school.

Plate 10

The Capsule

カプセル

The lead is a replaceable unit. It is manufactured off-site, graded, and hung on the desk with four bolts. It has origin, grade, joint type, and time-to-live. After TTL it is scrap, even if the row remains.

Kurokawa: the capsule is not a coffee cup. It is a climate. A rupture — bad consent, a wrong origin, a number that does not answer — collapses the interior. The operator who docks a dead unit does not receive a “less good lead.” He receives a breach in the wall.

Dimensions of the identity unit follow Nakagin’s truck-legal box: 2.5 × 4. The circular window is origin made visible — you can see into the unit; you cannot pretend it is a sealed mystery. The four bolts are the joint. The rust edge is Joint — an invented coat: this object was finished to be weatherproof for a short life, not to be monumental.

Ka is the category of demand. Kata is the grade and joint. Katachi is the numbered capsule on a specific wall. Never sell katachi as if it were ka. “We have leads” is not a sentence we use.

Plate 11

The Joint

接合

The commercial moment is the post. Ping goes out with no PII. Buyers bid. The winner’s CRM gets the record. Exclusive is one dock, higher CPL. Shared is two to five docks; if the first dial is after sixty seconds the shared lead is waste. Kurokawa’s en is the webhook: two systems meet without erasing each other.

Exclusive means a dedicated joint: the capsule hangs on one operator’s wall. Shared means a clustered hang: several operators may receive the same climate, and the grade and price must say so. Mixing exclusive and shared on one invoice line is a false bolt — it looks fastened and is not.

The umbilical carries consent, source, and routing. About one module long, like Nakagin’s flexible pipe. If the umbilical is hidden “for elegance,” the unit cannot be serviced. Hidden service is how capsules rot on the shaft.

The thirty-centimetre lesson: design the gap so a unit can leave without removing its neighbours. In contracts this means a kill switch that does not require rewriting the frame. In routing this means a capsule can be unscrewed from a buyer without taking the vertical down.

Homo movens, for us, is the operator: he moves among verticals and hours. The capsule must be able to move with him or leave him. Status is not a campus. Status is a working joint.

Plate 12

Diagram Language

図法

JAWNIX draws systems the way the 1960 booklet drew cities: plan, axonometric, annotated joint. Not dashboards. Not illustrated mascots. A diagram is a proposal for how something grows.

Elements

  • Core — rectangle, béton, two service lines. Always vertical unless the plate is a bay plan.
  • Capsule — 2.5 × 4, porthole, rust edge. Dashed = next / remnant.
  • Joint — Joint colour only. Four circles or a 4 px bar. Never a heart, never a node glow.
  • Umbilical — steel, one continuous curve, no arrows unless direction is the claim.
  • Helix — verdigris, thin, for data and growth. Slow. Never a decoration behind type.
  • Artificial land — a slab or waterline. Inventory sits on it; it is not a footer rule.

Line weights

Structure 1.2–1.6. Service 0.7. Dimension 0.5 in Formwork. Joint 1.1 in Joint. Type on drawings is Mono 7–11. No hairlines that vanish in print.

Sequence always includes scrap and next. A diagram that ends on “success” is advertising. A diagram that shows the empty bracket is metabolist.

Plate 14

Do / Do Not

可否

Do

  • Name what lasts and what turns on every public surface.
  • Grade the unit. Stamp origin, joint, and TTL.
  • Leave a flange: show unfinished capacity.
  • Draw joints as joints. Four bolts or a rust bar.
  • Write in articles and specifications.
  • Use Shippori, Plex Sans JP, Plex Mono only.
  • Grow pages by group form and 2.5 × 4 modules.
  • Design replacement so it can actually happen.
  • Keep Japanese as doctrine (新陳代謝), not as neon ornament.
  • Scrap expired tags. Empty brackets are honest.

Do not

  • Swiss red-black-white posters. Helvetica as hero. Twelve-column piety.
  • Metronomes, takt, musical notation, “the interval.”
  • Neon Tokyo, anime, cyberpunk, purple glow, glassmorphism.
  • Inter, Geist, Roboto, Arial, system-ui as the voice.
  • Emoji, Latin filler text, handshake photography.
  • Robot mascots, capsule-as-character, crypto-protocol diagrams.
  • Relabel remnant as Grade A. Hide the umbilical. Close the flange.
  • Say unlock, smash, journey, ecosystem, always-on.
  • Use the school as Japan-flavour. This is 1960 architecture, not a district.
  • Bounce. The dock is linear. Tissue does not bounce.
